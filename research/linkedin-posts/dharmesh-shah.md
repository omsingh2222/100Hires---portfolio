## Post 1

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dharmesh_woo-hoohubspot-just-launched-its-next-activity-7465429394967351296-ul9U

**Topic:** Launch announcement for HubSpot's new "Agent CLI" — reframed as a broader thesis on why software needs to be built for both human and AI agent users (UX + AX)

**Full post text:**
Woo hoo! HubSpot just launched it's next generation CLI (Command-Line Interface). We call it the HubSpot Agent CLI because it's built with agentic use in mind and also because we're clever at naming things. :)

It's in private beta, signup link in the comments (you should sign-up first and then read the rest of the post -- I'll wait).

So, here's what's going on... The future of software isn't humans or agents. It's humans and agents. Working together. Humans bring context, judgment, taste, creativity...and occasionally, coffee and donuts. Agents bring speed, scale, patience, and a shocking willingness to read documentation.

Here's the thing: Very technical and/or very quirky humans have been using command-line interfaces — CLIs — for decades. It's what I spend most of my day in. This is also the interface that every fictional character using a computer in a movie seems to prefer too. Why? Because for many jobs, the CLI is simply the fastest, cleanest, most efficient way to get things done inside a system. No hunting through menus. No clicking through seventeen screens. No "where did they move that setting?" existential crisis. Just type magic mantras learned over a long period of time and bang!, it's done.

Historically, software companies have focused a lot on UX: user experience. And they should. Great UX matters. But in the modern era, the systems that win will need something else too: AX. Agentic experience. How good is your system at being used by agents? Can agents understand it? Navigate it? Act inside it safely and efficiently?

Agents can access systems through APIs, MCP, and yes — CLIs. In fact, modern agentic systems like Claude Cowork and OpenAI's Codex love CLIs.

So, back to the announcement. HubSpot just announced its next-generation Agent CLI today (it's in private beta). But we didn't just take all of our existing APIs, wrap them in a command-line interface, and call it a day. That would have been the easy thing. We want to make things easy for our users (both human and agentic) not just ourselves.

So, we're taking a bit of a different path. CLIs have historically been built for human developers. As a human developer myself, I think that's great. But what if we reimagined the CLI from an agent's perspective? How would they want it to work? What would make it easier, faster, safer, and more useful for an AI agent trying to help a customer grow better? That's what we're working on at HubSpot.

Because the next generation of software won't just be designed for humans clicking around. It will be designed for humans and agents working together. UX + AX. Because AI needs its own UI. That's the new bar and a step towards the vision Duncan Lennox and team are building towards: the agentic customer platform.

All feedback appreciated. Thanks!

**Why this post matters:**
This is a textbook example of a founder turning a product launch into a thought-leadership post rather than a dry announcement. The product news (Agent CLI) is wrapped inside a bigger, more shareable idea ("UX + AX"), which is what makes it resonate beyond just HubSpot's existing customer base. It also shows the pattern of acknowledging competitor/peer tools by name (Claude Cowork, Codex) rather than pretending they don't exist — a credibility signal for a technical B2B SaaS audience. Strong structural pattern: hook, reframe, technical explanation, callback to the hook at the end.

**Topic:** A short, punchy explainer on why the "harness" around an AI model (tools, memory, skills, context) matters more than the underlying model itself

**Full post text:**
The AI harness matters more than the model.

Models have gotten really good. Great reasoning, large context windows, better instruction following.

But, what makes use of those capabilities is actually the harness. It's what provides tools, memory, skills and context to the model.

ChatGPT is a harness. Claude Cowork is a harness.

Without the harness, the model is just an engine with no car. You don't get anywhere.

## Post 3

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dharmesh_as-it-stands-and-likely-for-a-short-time-activity-7464346800255029248-CHka

**Topic:** A forward-looking prediction that AI agents will increasingly become the actual buyers/evaluators of B2B software platforms, not just users — and how that changes vendor strategy

**Full post text:**
As it stands, and likely for a short time longer, a human will pick the customer platform (CRM) that will be used by the company. If the company has some users that will need to access the system across Android/iOS -- in addition to a regular web browser, they'll pick one that has a good mobile app. If the company has users that work in multiple languages, that will be a consideration in the selection. They'll pick one that supports multiple languages.

And now, if the company has some users that are human and some that are AI agents, they'll pick the customer platform that has a great UX to serve humans and a great AX (agentic experience) that serves agents.

In the future, an agent will help with the purchasing decision (or maybe even actually make it). And, it's not going to just go down a checklist that includes "has API and MCP support". It's going to check each platform provider against a specific set of endpoints/tools based on the company's needs. It's going to check online forums where other agents are posting to see which platforms have an efficient, well conceived Agent Experience. And, because we'll have human users for a while too, it will assess the platform for its ease-of-use too.

Agents will be discerning and diligent influencers/buyers of customer platforms. Software vendors won't just be able to put a "headless" label on their website and expect that to be sufficient. The agent will know which platforms were designed for them. Just like in the prior generation, humans knew which platforms were actually "easy to use" (which all software claims to be).

I'm spending all of my time now with the HubSpot team working on how to create the best agentic customer platform on the planet. So that when you "ask your agent" what customer platform best fits for your company, the choice will be clear because HubSpot is compelling.
