**Topic:** A contrarian take arguing that AI and agents cannot fix a fundamentally broken go-to-market engine, and that foundational marketing problems must be solved first before AI can act as an accelerant

**Full post text:**
Unpopular take: AI and Agents will not fix a broken GTM engine and business model.

Every week, I talk to 15-20 CEOs and Investors looking to scale their businesses and I hear them talking about AI like a silver bullet for their business when they have far more pressing issues. A lot of these companies: are spending too much of their budget on customers who don't stay with them, don't have strong positioning, product marketing, sales enablement or websites in place, don't have demand gen programs in place to find more of their ICP, and don't have content for all stages of their buyer journey.

Without these foundational elements in place, AI initiatives will have a hard time making an impact on any business. Once you have clarity on core strategic priorities, then AI can act as an accelerant.

**Why this post matters:**
This is a strong "unpopular take" hook format, directly challenging the dominant industry narrative (AI as a cure-all) at a time when most B2B SaaS content is enthusiastically pro-AI. The credibility comes from a specific, repeated data point (15-20 CEO/investor conversations per week) rather than a vague "I've noticed" claim. The closing line reframes the argument constructively — AI isn't dismissed entirely, it's sequenced correctly (foundation first, then AI as accelerant) — which avoids sounding purely anti-AI.

## Post 2

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shiv-n22_how-to-saas-turned-7-years-old-yesterday-activity-7467589505655525376-3F47

**Topic:** A 7-year company anniversary reflection breaking down the 7 biggest lessons learned building a profitable, bootstrapped B2B marketing consultancy with no outside investors

**Full post text:**
How To SaaS turned 7 years old yesterday. I started this business in 2019, leaving a C-Suite role in a PE-backed business and taking a huge leap into entrepreneurship. Since then, we've grown HTS into a profitable, multi 7-figure business with almost 20 employees. All bootstrapped, with no outside investors. Here are my 7 biggest lessons on this journey, especially from the last year:

1) Build businesses around real market problems you have a unique insight into. I started HTS having been a former CMO, having been in a business that was sold to Private Equity two times. Through that process, I learned so much about B2B companies, building marketing engines and what PE investors and CEOs care about. HTS is simply the expanded version of that unique insight and journey.

2) Real market problems never go away, no matter what is happening in the world. Since 2019, we've seen a global pandemic, sky-high valuations followed by a sharp decline in exit multiples, the rise of AI and much more. What hasn't changed? Companies every year encounter the same marketing problems. They need more pipeline, they need more revenue and they need the right people.

3) Double down on the core business, stay away from distractions. In the last 7 years, HTS has launched a lot of content, a course, a podcast, a software and other ancillary services. What's stuck? The stuff that helps us build our core business of helping companies and PE investors navigate their biggest GTM challenges. Every time we've bet on that, we've grown faster. Every time we've bet on something else, we've lost money.

4) Cash flow is king. From Year 1 of the business, I had a simple rule: we need 12 months of cash flow on hand at all times. This allowed us to take more risks and not worry about making payroll at any point. We stayed disciplined, never over-hired and never had any layoffs. In the last 7 years, we've had less than 10 people leave or get fired.

5) Commit to supporting the people. Part of our low employee attrition has been our commitment to giving people expanded roles. Even during slow periods, we've taken a short-term cash flow hit to keep the band together. This has paid huge dividends as the business has grown. Bet on people and they will bet on you.

6) Hold people accountable to being A-Players. Betting on people also means you have to push them to be the best versions of themselves. People have to grow with the business or the business starts to become stagnant. If the business becomes stagnant, people have nowhere to grow into. It becomes a vicious cycle.

7) Keep evolving the business. HTS first started with Marketing Strategy Consulting. Then Marketing Due Diligence. Then Fractional CMO services. Now we do Agency work plus Agentic/AI Marketing buildouts. Along the way, I've written 3 books and scaled our podcast into a top 3 PE podcast. This evolution never stops. You have to stay tuned in to your market and customers and continue evolving how you are bringing them value.

**Why this post matters:**

Useful as a primary source for understanding how a B2B SaaS consultancy founder thinks about sustainable, bootstrapped growth versus the more commonly discussed VC-backed growth model.

## Post 3

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shiv-n22_a-lot-of-companies-and-websites-right-now-activity-7390015948734308354-iqGV

**Topic:** A critique of companies using "AI-Powered" as their primary above-the-fold marketing message, arguing AI should be framed as a means to an outcome, not the outcome itself — with before/after copy examples

**Full post text:**
A lot of companies and websites right now have "AI-Powered" above the fold in a way that makes it seem like it is the main benefit that comes from buying the solution. At least that's what it feels like as a customer, prospect and buyer. Nothing could be further from the truth.

AI is the vehicle, not the destination. Just like social is a vehicle. Just like mobile is a vehicle. Just like websites are a vehicle.

Instead, focus on the transformation you provide: "All-in-one hub to sell anywhere" is better than "The AI-Powered commerce platform." "Close deals faster with sales intelligence" is better than "AI-Powered sales insights." "Get more projects done in record time" is better than "AI-Powered project management."

Again, AI is the vehicle, not the destination. If you want to add AI as one of the ways you deliver on your promise, that's fine. If you want to say your solution helps customers deliver better results in an AI world, that's fine too. Just don't make it seem like that's the main benefit of working with you, because it's not.

**Why this post matters:**
This post pairs directly with Talya Heller's positioning-focused content and Natalie Marcotullio's "AI copy fatigue" post elsewhere in this research, showing the same underlying industry concern (overused, generic AI messaging) from a third independent voice — reinforcing this as a genuine, widely-recognized trend rather than one person's opinion. The "vehicle, not destination" metaphor is simple, memorable, and reusable, and the three side-by-side before/after copy examples make the advice immediately actionable for a marketer's own website or deck.
