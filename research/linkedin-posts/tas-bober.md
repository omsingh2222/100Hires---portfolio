## Post 2

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tasbober_what-if-there-was-a-b2b-event-that-was-just-activity-7467981341649711104-7JbT

**Topic:** Pitching a new B2B marketing conference concept specifically for solo consultants, freelancers, and agencies — a community segment most B2B events ignore — and asking the audience for direct validation before building it

**Full post text:**
What if there was a B2B event that was just for us outcasts of B2B - the solos, fracs, consultants, and agencies? Would you attend?

Most B2B marketing events don't want us in the room. And I get it. We aren't the ICP, so I'm not throwing shade. But based on the feedback from The Marketers Exit podcast, the sheer number of layoffs in tech/marketing, and the knowledge gap of doing your own thing, I think there's a lot here. I met with Andy Milligan yesterday to talk about event ideas in Columbus in 2027 Spring/Summer and we were most excited about this one.

Here's what we're thinking: smaller numbers (sub 100 attendees, understanding what's actually causing low attendance at events - cost, ICP, ROI, food, learnings - and reducing that friction); knowledge segments (pricing, offers, sales calls, getting clients, from people doing this well or who've failed enough to know what bad looks like); a "client therapy happy hour" (sharing worst client stories on stage, something that worked well at a past event called Above the Fold); sponsors where this audience is actually the ICP or has influence over the ICP (naming specific tools she personally uses); and food and drink "on fleek" referencing a past event's catering as the bar to beat.

The most important point: attainability. The more sponsors locked in, the cheaper tickets can be. Goal is under $500 so people don't need company approval to attend.

Point is - we're not all completely useless. There are tools I recommend to clients during every engagement. They sign up without blinking an eye because they trust my referrals. I've worked with over 50 companies. Another freelancer I know is one of the largest clients for a well known platform in our space because she makes all her clients purchase it.

We're still noodling on format. Open to your thoughts. But first things first - would you actually show up to something like this?

**Why this post matters:**
This post is a clear example of "building an audience before building the product" — she's validating demand for a new event concept publicly before committing resources to it, using the LinkedIn audience itself as a research tool. It's a long, detailed post with a numbered breakdown of the concept, but it's anchored by a direct yes/no question at both the start and end, which makes engagement easy even for people who don't read the whole thing. Also notable: she pushes back on a common industry stereotype (that solo consultants/freelancers don't hold buying influence) with two concrete counter-examples, turning a defensive point into a persuasive one.
