## Post 1

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/retentionadam_agency-owner-do-i-need-75-moltsets-api-keys-activity-7476296469281562624-vo90

**Topic:** A dialogue-style post explaining a new pricing model for his startup MoltSets (one API key instead of per-seat pricing), framed as a Q&A between Adam and a skeptical agency owner

**Full post text:**
Agency Owner: Do I need 75 MoltSets API keys for my 75 clients?
Me: No, you only need 1. RUN ME OVER.

Agency Owner: You're insane. Why would you not charge me per customer?
Me: There are two main reasons: 1. I'm trying to ABSOLUTELY DOMINATE the AI/Agentic use case (and do nothing else) 2. I think the seat and credit-based pricing model for contact data needs to get wrecked

Agency Owner: What does the Agentic use case have anything to do with charging me for 75 API keys?
Me: Agents want APIs ONLY (or MCP). I believe their HUMANS want high quality data at an extremely low, fixed price to avoid unpredictable cost loops. I can't try to win the Agentic use case, then turn around and charge you for 75 keys when I can't stop people from abusing them anyways.

Agency Owner: Fair enough. How do I know your data quality isn't shit?
Me: Try it. 1,000 free calls. I guarantee you coverage and quality will be as good or better than whatever alternative you're using now.

Agency Owner: Did I hear something about "100% valid data?" What does that mean?
Me: We append a "date of last validation" to every email address we pass you, and verify mobile numbers through the carriers in real time.

Agency Owner: How's the coverage compare to Apollo?
Me: We have a waterfall (like Clay does it) that fills in contacts for profiles that we miss. Coverage should be comparable or better.

Agency Owner: If I can do all this on 1 API key, it's like a 97% cost reduction for what I'm spending on data.
Me: LIKE I SAID … RUN ME OVER!!!

Agency Owner: I think I will!!!

Sign up for Moltsets waitlist here. Beta starts soon.

**Why this post matters:**
This is a distinctive format — an entire post written as an imagined back-and-forth conversation rather than a standard narrative or list post. It lets Adam pre-empt every objection a skeptical buyer would raise (pricing, data quality, coverage vs competitors) inside the post itself, which is a smart way to do bottom-of-funnel selling disguised as content. The repeated "RUN ME OVER" line also functions as a memorable, quotable hook that's likely to drive comments. Useful example of format variety beyond standard "story" or "listicle" posts.

## Post 2

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/retentionadam_over-the-past-10-years-ive-bootstrapped-activity-7457491668079112192-Jw7t

**Topic:** A founder retrospective comparing the build timelines, team sizes, and outcomes of his four bootstrapped startups, ending in a motivational takeaway about the compounding skill of repeated founding

**Full post text:**
Over the past 10 years, I've bootstrapped startups $0-$1M three times, and I'm about to do it a 4th. Each time was TWICE as fast with HALF the resources. Here's the breakdown of what I did, how long it took, how many FTEs we had, and what's coming next:

1. Robly Email Marketing — January 2014 - May 2015. Took 17 months, staff of 25 when we hit it. Entirely sales-led. We had a call center to book a $35/mo subscription and did a 1-to-1 manual onboarding (it was a really, really good list). Stalled out at $3m ARR, sold to PE in 2020 for $10m.

2. GetEmails (now Retention.com) — November 2019 - April 2020. Took 27 weeks, staff of 6 when we hit it. Did Facebook ads, drove a 15min demo. Diana Ross did thousands of them. Currently $21.7m ARR, 27% total margin 4.5 years later.

3. RB2B — March 2022 - June 2022. Took 16 weeks, staff of 5 when we hit it. Founder-brand driven inbound-led outbound (mostly LinkedIn). We're at $8.4m ARR 2 years later, about to have only 1 person working on it full time.

4. MoltSets — June 2026 - ??? Agent-first positioning in a mature, saturated market. Founder-brand and UGC/creator GTM, PLG motion. We're starting with an FTE team of 4. We're doing non-subscription usage-based pricing (for the agents), so could be slower than RB2B.

TAKEAWAY: Startups are HARD. The longer you do this, the more skilled you become at the craft. We're all looking to capture lightning in a bottle. Each swing you take, you're closer to it. Keep building.

**Why this post matters:**
This is a very strong "receipts" post — specific numbers (ARR, timelines, team size, margins) for four different startups builds trust and credibility far more than vague claims of success. It also explicitly documents how his own go-to-market strategy evolved over a decade (sales-led to paid ads to founder-brand/LinkedIn to creator/PLG), which is directly useful as a primary source for understanding how a real operator thinks about channel strategy over time. The structured numbered format also makes it highly skimmable and saveable.
