## Post 1

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lashaylewis_anybody-that-tells-you-they-can-track-prompt-activity-7457414296210149376-oaKk

**Topic:** Why tracking "prompt volume" for LLM search is fundamentally flawed, and how to optimize content for AI search instead of traditional keyword search

**Full post text:**
Anybody that tells you they can track prompt volume is probably lying to you.

How we think about ranking is fundamentally shifting. Back when it was purely search engines it was easier to say… "Best project management software" gets 100 searches a month. You could imagine a subset of people typing in that exact 4 word phrase in.

But LLM searches are drastically different. They're contextual and conversational. Instead of "best project management software," it's now more like: "I'm managing a team of 5 marketers and I'm struggling to keep up with the influx of tasks around our marketing campaigns. Are there any tools that can help me better manage this?"

And here's the thing… that will probably be the only way someone ever describes their problem specifically that way. Dozens of characters. A specific problem. Which means searches are now going to be 1 of 1. And if that's the case, how are you able to track prompt volume?

So how am I optimizing for LLMs? I think about it like this:
- Group queries by topic
- Within those topics capture different angles
- Get as close to the actual query as I can by modeling customer language
- Expand my semantic territory
- Surface by proximity

You're probably never going to know exactly what your prospect searches. But the thing is… you don't really need to. Surface close enough to the actual query as you can. And rank by proximity.

This is a strong example of a contrarian, specific hook ("anybody who tells you X is lying") followed by a genuinely useful reframe of an industry assumption (keyword volume tracking) for the AI search era. It shows how to make a technical/strategic topic feel like an insider take rather than generic advice — relevant to understanding hook structures for B2B SaaS content.

## Post 2

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lashaylewis_i-dont-know-who-needs-to-hear-this-but-activity-7454532237607288832-hbsU

**Topic:** Debunking a common misconception about Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) and giving a practical 2-step framework for optimizing content for LLM citation

**Full post text:**
I don't know who needs to hear this, but optimizing for AEO doesn't mean add FAQ's in your content. I mean, that can be a piece of it. But AI search is smart enough to understand the context on the page. This means you don't have to explicitly have a question and answer style format for an LLM to cite you.

You should though, answer the questions your persona is asking throughout the content. Think they'd ask about a specific feature? Weave it in where it makes sense. What about a painful problem they deal with? Include that too, naturally throughout the content.

And where do you get this information on the questions they'd ask? Your sales calls.
- Parse the transcripts
- See what questions they're asking
- Position them as a query
- Answer it (with detail) in your content

There are LLM visibility tools charging upwards of $200/m for "content optimization". When you could actually optimize it way better with a simple two step flow. Sales call transcript -> LLM query positioning.

## Post 3

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lashaylewis_if-you-have-no-sales-call-data-but-need-activity-7439295086800072704-r-Sx

**Topic:** A complete interview guide for capturing customer language when sales call data isn't available, to support AI-search-optimized content

**Full post text:**
If you have no sales call data, but need to capture your customers' voice. Use this interview guide.

Every piece of content I create with AI's help is rooted in customer language. This typically comes from either: sales call transcripts or CS call transcripts. But the unfortunate reality is that most companies I speak with either don't record their sales calls at all, or the marketer can't access them. So if that's you, use this interview guide.

Interview current users and ask them these questions:

Role and context: Walk me through your role? What are you responsible for? What does a typical week look like? How is your success measured? Who do you report to? What do they care about most?

Problem before core product: Before [product] how were you handling [job-to-be-done]? What wasn't working about that approach? How much time did [problem] cost you per week? What was the moment you decided you HAD to find a solution? When you first started looking, what did you search for?

The decision: What mattered most when choosing a solution? Who else was involved in the decision? What almost stopped you from buying [product]? What convinced you to choose [product]?

Life with product: What changed after you started using [product]? What specific results have you seen? Which features do you use most? Why? If you had to convince a colleague, what would you say?

Language: In your own words, what does [product] do? How is [product] different from [competitor]? What's still frustrating about [product]?

Then take this transcript and use it to make a customer language artifact. That artifact is then taken into account when your content is being written. I mean what better way to increase your odds of showing up in AI search than using the language your customers are using when they query things there?
