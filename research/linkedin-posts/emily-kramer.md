## Post 2

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/emilykramer_intercom-fin-activity-7472311617482022912-xDcO

**Topic:** A real-time reaction to the Intercom/Fin acquisition by Salesforce, using a recent billboard observation to make a broader point about brand equity and the risks of confusing name changes or sub-branding

**Full post text:**
Intercom → Fin → Salesforce. Apparently, my taste in billboards does not correlate with acquisition interest.

Intercom/Fin announced its acquisition today, but on my recent SF billboard tour with Maya Spivak, I was less than impressed with their Fin billboards. I couldn't tell what was going on. What is that orange blob? Maya said it looked like a ball of yarn from a Disney short, probably not what they were going for.

Intercom used to run clean, plain-text ads as billboards. Those stood out for being boring, and got coverage for the simplicity. This creative lands in a weird middle: not as clear as the plain-text ads, not memorable enough to count as great creative.

The other problem here: the name change. It was unclear for a while if Intercom was now Fin, if Fin was just the agent, and I didn't even know an official name change happened in May until I read about the acquisition today.

So what's the lesson? Building brand equity takes a very long time. If you change your name or launch a sub-brand, use your existing brand credibility to bridge the gap. Don't make people guess who you are (unless, of course, you have real reasons to distance yourself from the old name). But maybe none of this matters, because Salesforce seems to be on a buying spree?! Maybe Benioff loves orange yarn?

Did the Intercom/Fin name thing confuse you over time too? Part 2 of my billboard newsletter, with more videos from the drive-by, is out in a few days.

**Why this post matters:**
This post is a strong example of "newsjacking" done well — reacting to a same-day acquisition announcement using firsthand, pre-existing material (her own billboard photos from a prior outing) rather than scrambling to create new content. The casual, slightly irreverent tone (orange yarn jokes, "Benioff loves orange yarn") makes a brand strategy lesson feel observational and fun rather than lecture-y, while still landing a genuinely useful, generalizable lesson about brand equity and naming changes. It also functions as a teaser for a longer newsletter, showing how she repurposes a single research trip (the billboard tour) into multiple pieces of content across formats.
