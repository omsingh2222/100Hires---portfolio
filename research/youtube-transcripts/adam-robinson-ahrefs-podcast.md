## Key takeaways

**1. Company brand lost to personal brand — even with an $800K domain.**
Adam spent $800,000 acquiring the Retention.com domain to build an "authoritative, scalable company brand." Company-driven content underperformed his personal storytelling anyway — the core lesson he repeats across appearances: social platforms reward people, not logos.

**2. The proof of concept: RB2B hit $5M ARR in 13 months with 4 people, zero paid spend.**
He treated RB2B as a live experiment to see if the LinkedIn-only playbook he'd built on Retention.com (which reached ~$13-25M ARR) could be repeated faster, leaner, with no outbound or ad spend — just daily founder posting.

**3. Why LinkedIn specifically.** Only roughly 2% of LinkedIn users actively post, which he frames as a low-competition, high-visibility opportunity compared to more saturated platforms like Twitter/X for B2B SaaS founders.

**4. Modeled his style after specific creators, not generic "best practices."**
He cites studying Tom Wolfe's polarizing-hook structure and Chris Walker's discipline of repeating the same handful of core truths through dozens of different formats and hooks instead of constantly inventing new ideas.

**5. Early traction came from listicle/problem-solution templates before he earned the right to do pure storytelling.**
Numbered, structured posts with embedded takeaways performed well early; deeper narrative storytelling came later once he'd built credibility and an audience read on what resonated.

**6. Video and live webinars as a differentiation layer.**
Beyond daily text posts, he runs a weekly live show with segments like "Pitch Slap" and "Unfuck My Startup," bringing on guests (Sam Parr, Jeremy Horwitz, Nathan Barry) — leaning on the unpredictability of live formats to break through LinkedIn's text-post monotony.
