## Key excerpts and takeaways

**On researching how technical buyers actually evaluate landing pages:**
She described going beyond marketing's own opinions to interview the actual buyers — directors of InfoSec, VPs of Compliance, CFOs, CTOs — with no stake in the outcome: "I had no skin in the game. So they were like, happy to talk to me because I was like, I'm just curious, and I just want to know how you buy software." — Tas Bober

**On what buyers actually said about testimonials and awards:**
"Yes, they want to see testimonials on sites. No, they don't care about your awards... some of them didn't even know what G2 was, which was shocking to me. And then finally they said, yeah, we like the testimonials on sites, but honestly, we don't think that they're real people." This directly challenges a common B2B landing page convention (badge walls, award logos) by going to the actual buyer source rather than assuming what works.

**Her "three-page test" (from the related Ultimate Roast episode):**
"I do this thing called a three-page test, which is how many pages does it take me on average to figure out what a company does? And the average is three. So if you're anywhere under three, congratulations." A simple, repeatable diagnostic tool she applies live when auditing landing pages.

**On separating homepages from landing pages:**
She emphasizes keeping homepages and campaign landing pages distinct to preserve data integrity and keep messaging tightly matched to the specific campaign driving traffic — rather than sending all paid traffic to a general-purpose homepage.
