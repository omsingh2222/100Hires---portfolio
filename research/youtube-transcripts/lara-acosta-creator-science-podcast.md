"Honestly, I don't know. I am the biggest pain to any coach that tries to coach me because they always go in, who's your target audience? What's your ICP? And I'm like, I don't know... I never had an ICP. I always just had a feeling, almost like a vibe of the person that I want to attract."

**[04:02] Lara Acosta, on her actual audience:**
"The one thing they all have in common is that they want to grow. And they are very curious about the internet. And they really like both a very professional style, but also quite relaxed."

**[07:00] Lara Acosta, on storytelling in every post:**
"Every single piece of content I ever write always has to have an ingrained story in it, because that's what always makes it feel like even if I'm saying the most basic type of information, it's mine, no one else's."

**[09:13] Lara Acosta, on crafting hooks:**
"The hook should look and feel like the thumbnail and title of a YouTube video that you want to watch. Think about it as a game of YouTube." (She recommends the first line of a LinkedIn post be 8-10 words to capture attention on mobile feeds.)

**[14:22] Lara Acosta, on visuals:**
"If a post on LinkedIn doesn't have an image attached to it, a good quality image as well, it's missing out on even performing better."

**[26:25] Lara Acosta, on video being overrated for LinkedIn growth:**
"Video for growth on LinkedIn is absolutely overrated and it actually doesn't do well for growth." She attributes this to LinkedIn's user base, which predominantly comprises professionals who prefer quick, text-based interactions over lengthy video content, and notes that while video helps personal branding, it does not necessarily contribute to audience growth specifically.

**[44:28] Lara Acosta, on motivation/why:**
"If you don't know why you're doing something, then it's not going to work because your heart isn't in it."

**[52:16] Lara Acosta, on personalized connection requests:**
"If I'm trying to connect with Jay, I would say... 'I loved your podcast with Karen Drew. It was so good... Thank you so much for putting this effort into your podcast.'" She emphasizes that tailored, specific connection request messages significantly increase acceptance rates compared to generic invites.

## Key takeaways for this research

1. **Direct contradiction worth noting:** Lara's strongest claim in this episode — that video is "absolutely overrated" for LinkedIn growth — runs counter to a lot of current 2026 industry messaging pushing creators toward video-first content. This is a valuable counter-signal for any LinkedIn content strategy playbook: text-and-image posts may still outperform video specifically for growth (vs. branding), according to someone with proven, large-scale results.

2. **No rigid ICP, but a clear "vibe":** Unlike a lot of B2B marketing advice that insists on a tightly defined ICP, Lara describes succeeding with more of a felt sense of who she's writing for (curious, growth-oriented, professional-but-relaxed) rather than a demographic-first approach. Useful nuance against more rigid frameworks elsewhere in this research.

3. **Hooks as "YouTube thumbnails":** Her mental model of treating the first line of a LinkedIn post like a YouTube thumbnail/title is a clean, transferable metaphor — directly reinforces the "first 3 lines should be a story" rule from her own SLAY framework documented in her LinkedIn posts file, but explains the underlying psychology (compelling someone to click "see more") behind why it works.

4. **No scheduled content calendar:** Contrary to many "post consistently on a schedule" frameworks common in B2B SaaS marketing advice, Lara describes writing reactively/daily rather than batching and scheduling — a notable contrast worth flagging if this research is eventually synthesized into a single playbook, since it shows there isn't one single "correct" operating process even among top creators.
