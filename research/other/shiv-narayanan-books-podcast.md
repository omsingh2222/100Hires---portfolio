## Methodology note
Episode 66 of his own podcast is unusual in that Shiv breaks format and explains his own content/GTM strategy directly to the audience, which gives a rare, explicit window into his thinking about why he built each piece of content (book vs. podcast vs. product) the way he did. Combined with book landing pages and a guest interview appearance. Quotes reproduced as short attributed fragments under 15 words per source.

## Key material

**Why he wrote two ICP-specific books instead of generic marketing books:**
"I could have written all kinds of marketing books, but I specifically focused on these because that is our ICP. I want founders who are ready to exit to read it. I want PE investors that are buying companies to read it."

**Why content needs a "vehicle" beyond one-time consumption (his product-vs-content distinction):**
He observed that on a typical SaaS site, the free trial form generates more revenue than any other lead form — including white paper downloads — because product has recurring value, while "you can only read a book once, you can only listen to a podcast once, you can only watch a YouTube video once," requiring constant new content production to sustain attention versus a product that compounds.

**Why he built a podcast around his buyer's problems, not his own offering:**
"We are a marketing services firm for private equity, but we built the podcast called the Private Equity Value Creation Podcast because it's not just about what we sell, it's about the problems of the ICP that we are serving." He deliberately avoids constant self-promotion on the show, instead positioning HTS as "entrusted with a responsibility to help this market" broadly — bringing on other service providers and PE firms to share best practices, not just selling.

**His three books, in sequence, reflecting his actual ICP journey:**
1. *Post-Acquisition Marketing* — how to build a marketing action plan in the first 100 days after a PE acquisition
2. *Exit-Ready Marketing* — a 9-element framework for what makes a company's marketing function valuable/scorecard-able to a future buyer
3. *AI Marketing Blueprint* — his newest book, described as "7 New Rules for CEOs and revenue leaders to drive pipeline and revenue in a world where AI has transformed the buyer journey"

## Why this matters for the project
This is the only source in the research set where an expert explicitly narrates his own content-distribution logic out loud (product vs. book vs. podcast, recurring value vs. one-time consumption) rather than the research team having to infer it. It substantially deepens the three LinkedIn posts already collected from Shiv (`research/linkedin-posts/shiv-narayanan.md`), which show the output of this strategy (his 7-year anniversary lessons post, his "AI is the vehicle not the destination" post) but not the underlying reasoning for why he chose these specific formats.

## Cross-expert connection
His "product has recurring value, content has to be constantly remade" observation is a sharper, more explicit version of the same content-economics thinking implicit in Natalie Marcotullio's interactive-demo strategy (a self-serve product experience that scales without constant new content) and in Adam Robinson's pivot from a one-time $800K domain purchase to ongoing daily founder content.
