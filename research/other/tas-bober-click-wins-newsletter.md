# Tas Bober — "Click Wins" Newsletter (The Scroll Lab)

**Source:** Tas Bober
**Type:** Newsletter (beehiiv)
**Publication:** Click Wins
**Link:** newsletter.thescrolllab.com
**Date:** Launched mid-2026
**Format:** Weekly micro-newsletter, one landing page tip per issue

## Summary / Key excerpt

Click Wins is Tas Bober's newly launched "micro-newsletter," deliberately designed around a single constraint: one landing page tip or learning delivered to the inbox per week, rather than a longer, more traditional newsletter format. She explained the format choice directly in her launch announcement: as a mom of two and business owner, she wanted something "easy to consume and selfishly easier to commit to" for herself as the writer, not just the reader.

Her credibility framing for the newsletter is notably blunt and specific rather than vague: she states she has worked with 50 B2B companies on their landing pages in the last 3 years through her consulting business, previously led 3 in-house digital and website teams, and has been part of managing 400 websites across her career. She summarizes this experience with the self-deprecating line "I've f'ed up A LOT" — positioning the newsletter's value as hard-won, mistake-driven knowledge rather than theoretical best practices.

A separate, related LinkedIn post describes her actual content production workflow that feeds this newsletter: she consolidated what used to be 5+ scattered idea-capture tools (emails to herself, texts to herself, Google Keep, Apple Notes, Coda) into a single Ideas doc in Coda, then built a custom AI "skill" that extracts talking points from that ideas doc plus her call transcripts (via Fathom) and podcast transcripts, organizing them by source, topic theme, and how each idea ties into her point of view.

## Relevance

This is directly useful as supporting material for the LinkedIn organic content strategy research topic, for two reasons. First, it's a real, currently-active example of a B2B SaaS practitioner intentionally choosing a constrained content format (one-tip, weekly, micro-newsletter) specifically to sustain consistency as a solo creator — a practical, less commonly discussed alternative to the more typical advice of "post long-form content regularly." Second, her described AI-assisted content workflow (consolidating idea sources, then using an AI skill to extract and organize talking points before writing) is a concrete, real-world example of the same category of AI tooling (Claude Code skills, in her case applied to her own content pipeline) that this entire 100Hires research project is built around — directly reinforcing patterns already seen in Emily Kramer's and Lashay Lewis's content elsewhere in this research, where multiple experts independently describe building custom AI workflows to support their own content creation process rather than writing from scratch each time.
